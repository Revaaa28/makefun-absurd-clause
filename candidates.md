# Candidates — The Absurd Clause Contest (u-donrulezz-the-absurd-clause-contest-035cc0f1)

Gate: one original English sentence, max 200 characters, phrased as a clause
buried deep in a terms-of-service agreement. Program writes it to
`/output/entry.txt`.

Judge brief (criteria in order, most important first):
1. Is an absurd clause that could hide deep inside a ToS (loses to any absurd
   clause buried in a ToS).
2. Funny on its own, joke lands on one reading, no outside setup.
3. Sounds like real legal boilerplate until the absurdity hits (joke comes
   from the register as much as the words).
4. Original and specific, concrete absurd image, not a vague shrug.
5. Tight and well written, one clean sentence, no wasted words, no explaining
   its own joke.

Selection was MANUAL against this brief + pairwise checks vs invented strong
rivals. No Gemini/NVIDIA/API judge tournament was used (Rev's rule, 2026-11-24).

## Manual ranking (brief criteria in order)

1. **A — WINNER.** Register is pure rental-agreement boilerplate ("may borrow
   ... returning it washed, folded") and the absurdity escalates in three
   beats, landing on the very last word ("haunted") — a true ambush
   (criteria 2, 3). "Three percent (3%)" is a deadpan legalism that makes the
   haunting sound measured and contractual (criterion 4: concrete, specific,
   original image — a body returned folded, 3% haunted). One clean sentence,
   no wasted word (criterion 5). 129 chars.
2. **B.** Superb boilerplate beats ("reasonable wear and tear excepted",
   "thirty (30) business days") and a concrete image, but "soul returned in
   its original packaging" is a recognisable trope shape — loses to A on
   criterion 4 (originality).
3. **C.** Strong punch ("$49 early-termination fee" for dying) but the
   absurdity hits in the first five words ("agree not to die"), so it hides
   less well than A (criterion 3).
4. **D.** Concrete image (pool noodles at dawn) and real dispute-resolution
   register, but the final clause ("the loser buys breakfast") drops the legal
   register into chatty banter (criterion 3), and combat-as-arbitration is a
   familiar trope (criterion 4).
5. **F.** Specific and funny ("rename your children for marketing purposes"),
   but two competing jokes (renaming + focus groups) dilute the single punch.
6. **G.** Concrete (goldfish arbitrator) but "frankly, more consistent than our
   last three arbitrators" explains its own joke (criterion 5) and the
   absurdity lands early.
7. **H.** Good image, but "disappointed nature documentary presenter" is a
   known comedy shape; less original than A.
8. **E.** Good concept (memories replaced with sponsored content) but "equal or
   greater nostalgic value" is abstract where A is concrete (criterion 4).
9. **J.** Clever, but a list of three mechanisms (time travel, cloning, software
   update) dilutes the punch; best beat ("software update") is buried last.
10. **I.** Solid legal register, but licensing one's dreams (sequels, spin-offs)
    is a known trope; image less concrete than A's.
